Stage 1: Lead-in & Context 10 minutes

Why do we use the Past Perfect here?

1. EARLIER PAST

"...allegations that he had performed incantations."

→
2. MAIN PAST

"Youth from his community came to his house."

→
3. LATER PAST

"Leo's organisation reassured Livinus."

Key Idea: Just like in storytelling, when we report what people said or claimed in the past, we use the Past Perfect to show an action happened before they spoke!

Stage 2A: Guided Discovery — Backshifting 10 minutes

Compare Direct Speech vs. Reported Speech

Look at these two examples based on Text 3. What changes when we report them?

Direct Speech (Original Words)

1. Livinus: "I didn't do any such thing!"

2. The group: "You have done nothing wrong."

Reported Speech (From Text 3)

1. Livinus responded that he had done no such thing.

2. They reassured Livinus that he had done nothing wrong.

1. Pronouns changed: I → he, You → he.
2. Verb tenses stepped back one tense ("Backshifting"): Both Past Simple (didn't do) and Present Perfect (have done) shifted to Past Perfect (had done)!
Stage 2A: Backshifting Rules 10 minutes

How Backshifting Works

Direct Speech → Reported Speech (after a past verb) Example
Present Simple → Past Simple "I live here." → He said he lived there.
Present Continuous → Past Continuous "You are copying." → They said he was copying.
Past Simple / Present Perfect → Past Perfect "I took / have taken it." → She said she had taken it.
will / can → would / could "We will help." → They said they would help.
Time & Place Words Shift Too: yesterday → the day before  |  today → that day  |  now → then  |  here → there

Stage 2B: Common Low-B2 Traps Check

Watch Out! Spot and Fix These 3 Errors

1. ❌ They reassured that he was safe.

✅ They reassured him / Livinus that he was safe. (reassure, urge, and warn NEED a person!)

2. ❌ His critics accused him to try to copy Western ideas.

✅ His critics accused him of trying to copy Western ideas. (accuse + someone + of + -ing)

3. ❌ The detective warned us to not touch anything.

✅ The detective warned us not to touch anything. ("not" goes BEFORE "to")
Stage 3: Controlled Practice — B2 First Part 4 20 minutes

Key Word Transformation: Golden Rules

Complete the second sentence so that it has a similar meaning to the first sentence, using the word given.

Rule 1

Do NOT change the key word in CAPITALS.

Rule 2

Use between 2 and 5 words only (including the key word).

Rule 3

Contractions (e.g., hadn't, didn't) count as TWO words!

Tip: Ask yourself two questions for every sentence:
  1. Which verb pattern does this word use? (that / to / of + -ing)
  2. Do I need to backshift the tense?

Stage 4: Speaking — Detective & The Silly Crime 30 minutes (2 Rounds)

Mission Briefing: How It Works

1. The Victim

Gets a secret Silly Crime prompt (1–2 mins to invent details). Tells the Detective what happened using direct/active speech.

2. The Detective

Listens carefully, asks 2–3 follow-up questions, and writes short keyword notes (no full sentences!).

3. Chief Inspector (Teacher)

The Detective reports the case to the Teacher using reported speech, backshifting, and 3+ reporting verbs!

Victim Fact-Check Rule: Stand silently next to your Detective while they report to the Chief Inspector. If they get a detail of your story wrong, cough dramatically or shake your head! (Then swap roles for Round 2.)
Stage 4: Storytelling & Reporting Toolkit Keep on Screen During Prep

Victim's Invention Checklist

Use your imagination to add these 4 details:

  • When & where did it happen?
  • Who do you suspect / accuse?
  • What did the suspect (or a witness) say to you?
  • What do you want the detective to do?

Detective's Reporting Toolkit

Try to include at least 3 of these when reporting to the Chief Inspector:

  • "Chief, he/she explained that..." (+ Past Perfect!)
  • "He/she accused [someone] of + -ing..."
  • "The suspect denied + -ing / responded that..."
  • "A friend reassured [him/her] that..."
  • "He/she urged us to... / warned us not to..."
Stage 4: Silly Crime Prompts (Cases 1–6) Click any card to zoom

1. The Vanishing Left Socks

Situation: All the left socks from your laundry disappeared overnight, while every right sock was left neatly folded.

Idea: Maybe a strange neighbour is collecting them for an art project, or an animal only likes left feet.

2. The Great Spaghetti Swap

Situation: You left your homemade pasta sauce on the stove for 10 minutes, and someone replaced it with cheap ketchup (or strawberry jam).

Idea: Maybe a jealous roommate ruined it on purpose, or someone tried to "improve" your recipe.

3. The Kidnapped Garden Gnome

Situation: Your beloved garden gnome (or lucky desk figurine) is missing, and you found a ransom note demanding 500g of expensive cheese.

Idea: Maybe a neighbour hated looking at it, or your friends are playing an elaborate prank.

4. The Mystery of the Ugly Mug

Situation: Your favourite coffee mug vanished from the shared kitchen, and a broken, dirty plastic cup was left in its exact spot.

Idea: Maybe a colleague/classmate broke yours by accident and hid the evidence, or really wanted your mug.

5. The Unauthorized Makeover

Situation: You fell asleep on the sofa for an hour, and woke up to find someone had painted your nails neon green (or cut a piece of your hair).

Idea: Maybe a flatmate lost a bet, or a sibling wanted to test out a new look on you.

6. The Wi-Fi Sabotage

Situation: Someone changed your home Wi-Fi name to "CleanTheKitchen_Loser" and changed the password so you cannot get online.

Idea: Maybe an annoyed roommate wanted revenge for dirty dishes, or a neighbour guessed your old password.

Stage 4: Silly Crime Prompts (Cases 7–12) Click any card to zoom

7. The Midnight Banana Bandit

Situation: Every morning this week, you have found five fresh bananas mysteriously placed inside your shoes, backpack, or car.

Idea: Maybe an overly generous neighbour thinks you look hungry, or someone at university/work is messing with you.

8. The Living Room Castle

Situation: You came home to find every single chair, table, and cushion in your living room built into a giant fort, and nobody is inside.

Idea: Maybe your roommates had a secret party, or someone climbed in through the window just to build a castle.

9. The Fake Review

Situation: Someone secretly used your unlocked phone to post a dramatic 1-star review (or an embarrassing love poem) on your favourite pizzeria's website.

Idea: Maybe a friend thinks they are a comedian, or a rival café worker borrowed your phone.

10. The Frozen Keys

Situation: You were already late to leave the house, and you discovered your keys frozen solid inside a giant block of ice in the freezer.

Idea: Maybe your partner or roommate didn't want you to leave without doing a chore, or it was a prank gone too far.

11. The Talkative Parrot

Situation: You looked after a friend's parrot for the weekend, and now it won't stop screaming your embarrassing secrets (or bank PIN) out the window.

Idea: Maybe someone taught it those words while you were in the shower, or it overheard a private phone call.

12. The Pasta Earbud

Situation: Someone stole only your right wireless earbud and replaced it inside the charging case with a single piece of dry macaroni.

Idea: Maybe a classmate wanted to annoy you before a study session, or a clumsy friend broke it and panicked.
